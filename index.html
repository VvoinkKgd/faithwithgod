Who is God?

Catholics believe there is one God in three Persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit — the Holy Trinity.

✝️

Who is Jesus?

Jesus Christ is the Son of God. Christians believe he became human, taught us how to love, died, and rose again.

🕊️

The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Trinity, who guides, strengthens, and helps the Church.

Prayers

Prayer is a way to speak with God. You can pray in your own words, too.

The Our Father

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

Hail Mary

Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

Glory Be

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

A simple prayer

Dear God, thank you for this day. Help me to be kind, patient, and loving. Guide me to make good choices and help people who need care. Amen.

The Ten Commandments

The commandments teach people to love God and respect others. They are found in Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5.

They include worshiping God alone, honoring parents, keeping the Lord's day holy, and not stealing or lying.

The Mass

The Liturgical Year

The Church year includes seasons such as Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter, and Ordinary Time. Each season helps Catholics reflect on Jesus' life and teachings.

Works of Mercy

Feed the hungry, welcome the stranger, visit the sick, comfort those who are sad, forgive, and care for people in need.

What Catholics believe

Catholics profess the Apostles' Creed and the Nicene Creed, which summarize central beliefs about God, Jesus, the Holy Spirit, the Church, and eternal life.

Saints and their stories

Saints are people the Church recognizes as holy examples of faith and love.

St. Francis of Assisi

Known for his love of creation, care for the poor, and simple life devoted to God.

St. Thérèse of Lisieux

Remembered for her “Little Way”: showing great love through small, everyday actions.

St. Martin de Porres

Known for humility, kindness, and caring for people who were sick or poor.

St. Joan of Arc

A young French woman remembered for her courage and faith during a difficult time in history.

St. Carlo Acutis

A young Catholic known for his love of the Eucharist and his interest in sharing information about the faith.

St. Teresa of Calcutta

Dedicated her life to serving people who were poor, sick, and in need.

A traditional Catholic devotion

Rosary guide

The Rosary is a prayerful meditation on events in the lives of Jesus and Mary.

How to begin

1. Sign of the Cross
Begin in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.
2. Apostles' Creed
Pray the Apostles' Creed.
3. Our Father
Pray one Our Father.
4. Three Hail Marys
Pray for faith, hope, and charity.
5. Glory Be
Pray the Glory Be.

The five decades

For each decade, announce a mystery, pray one Our Father, ten Hail Marys, and one Glory Be. Many people also pray the Fatima Prayer.

  • Joyful Mysteries: Monday and Saturday
  • Sorrowful Mysteries: Tuesday and Friday
  • Glorious Mysteries: Wednesday and Sunday
  • Luminous Mysteries: Thursday

The Rosary is a devotion, not a replacement for the Mass or the sacraments.

Questions about Catholicism

Some common questions and simple answers.

Why do Catholics pray?

Catholics pray to praise God, give thanks, ask for help, say sorry, and grow in relationship with God.

Why do Catholics go to Mass?

At Mass, Catholics gather as a community, listen to God's word, and celebrate the Eucharist.

Do Catholics worship Mary and the saints?

No. Catholics worship God alone. They honor Mary and the saints and may ask them to pray to God for us.

What is the Bible?

The Bible is the collection of sacred books Christians receive as the word of God. The Catholic Bible includes 73 books.

What if I have a question this site doesn't answer?

Ask a trusted parent, priest, catechist, or teacher. They can help explain Catholic teaching and answer personal questions.

      The Ten Commandments

      1. I am the Lord your God; you shall have no other gods before me.
      2. You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain.

      9. You shall not covet your neighbor's spouse.

      Wording and numbering can vary between traditions and translations.

      Creeds of the Church

      Apostles' Creed: A concise statement of Christian belief traditionally associated with the early Church and used in Catholic prayer.

      Nicene Creed: A fuller statement of faith recited at Sunday Mass and on solemnities, expressing belief in the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

      Use the Creed in your parish missal or prayer book for the complete text.

      A short Catholic history timeline

      • 1st century: Jesus' ministry, death, and Resurrection; the apostles preach the Gospel.
      • c. 50: The Council of Jerusalem is described in Acts 15.
      • 313: The Edict of Milan grants religious toleration in the Roman Empire.
      • 325: The Council of Nicaea meets.
      • 1054: A major division develops between Eastern and Western Christianity.
      • 1545–1563: The Council of Trent takes place.
      • 1962–1965: The Second Vatican Council meets.
      • Today: Catholics live and worship in communities around the world.

      Vatican City

      Vatican City is an independent city-state surrounded by Rome, Italy. It is the home of the Pope and the central offices of the Catholic Church.

      Places to learn about: St. Peter's Basilica, St. Peter's Square, the Vatican Museums, and the Sistine Chapel.

      The Pope serves as the Bishop of Rome and shepherd of the worldwide Catholic Church.
